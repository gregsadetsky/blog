I looked around, and it seemed to me like there weren't enough LLM evals out there.

Haha, ha.

Okay, [so I made a new eval](https://lockstep.greg.technology/), (don't) sue me. Like making an album, it's just a thing that people should do at least once, at some point in their life and/or career. It's not a thing that's wrong to do.

I'm also unsure of why I made it. Beyond the obvious "because".

But yeah - during my work on the [Minivac 601 Simulator](https://minivac.greg.technology/) in the old year of 2025, I noticed that LLMs were interesting to watch (and fail) whenever they tried to follow / resolve the result of a logical circuit.

Like - you'd say, "Hey LLM, I have a few NAND gates, and they're connected so, and then at first, I'm going to put 0 1 1 1 across their inputs - tell me what are the first output values? And then as the values cycle through, what are the values again?"

LLMs (from 2025) would often go into wild crazes while trying to solve this, poorly attempting to follow a flip flop through its fl(i/o)ps (flip flop is an incredible clown duo name btw.)

So yeah - in 2026, LLMs are less nutty about this. Also - why would you (or I) even ask an LLM to "solve" logical gate circuits? Well... I really do think it's interesting to see them struggle and try to remember/figure out "where they are". And it's also somewhat easy to make new circuits that are just hard enough to make all LLMs fail. So, it's parameterizable and ostensibly useful and correlated with other things that LLMs try to do - that's good? Maybe.

Have fun reading about it and talking about it, tweaking your new model to be better at it, building a startup around it, or maybe considering me [for you know what](https://www.nobelprize.org/)... haha!

Be well!
